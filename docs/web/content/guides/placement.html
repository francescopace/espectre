Place your ESPectre sensor

Distance, antenna orientation, obstacles, and a short room test to find a spot that detects movement and ignores noise.

Think of a radio link, not a detection cone

ESPectre does not watch a circle around the board. The access point's signal reaches the ESP32 directly and through many reflections off walls, furniture, and people. Motion is easiest to detect when it changes several of these paths, so the positions of the access point and the sensor, and the route people take, all matter.

Placement matters more than small threshold changes. Find a good spot first, then calibrate and tune.

A good starting placement

  • Put the ESP32 in the area you want to monitor, about 3–8 m from the access point if the layout allows it.
  • Choose a spot where people normally walk between the access point and the sensor, not only behind one of them.
  • Keep the antenna end of the board open to the room, away from metal, electronics, and large obstacles.
  • Mount it firmly. A board or cable that swings, vibrates, or gets moved during cleaning causes false motion on its own.
  • Make sure the sensor gets steady traffic, and settle the placement before changing detector settings.

Distance: use RSSI as the final check

Distances are only a starting point: walls, router power, antennas, and furniture all change the link. Use the Wi-Fi signal strength (RSSI) to check the result. ESPHome has a WiFi RSSI diagnostic sensor, and Live motion shows it under Diagnostics.

How to interpret the Wi-Fi RSSI observed at the sensor
Observed RSSIWhat it suggestsWhat to do
Above -40 dBmThe receiver may be too close to the access point.Move the sensor farther away before raising thresholds.
-40 to -70 dBmA good range for a first installation.Run the quiet-and-motion test below.
-70 to -80 dBmThe link is weaker, and placement matters more.Test carefully, and prefer a clearer path if results vary.
Below -80 dBmThe signal is weak and more noise is likely.Move the sensor or access point, or remove a heavy obstacle.

Stronger is not always better. A very short link can work worse than a moderate, stable one that crosses the part of the room people use.

Angle and antenna orientation

There is no single best angle. The antenna type, the case, and nearby materials all change the result. Start with this test:

  1. Start clear and facing the link

    Keep the antenna end away from walls and the case, and point the device toward the access point. Keep an external antenna vertical.

  2. Check a quiet room

    Leave the room empty and still long enough to see whether the score stays stable.

  3. Walk the real route

    Walk through the doorway, corridor, or path you want to detect. Waving a hand next to the board is not enough.

  4. Rotate by 90 degrees

    Change only the orientation, recalibrate if you use Lightweight, and repeat the same walk. Keep the orientation where quiet and motion differ most.

The comparison matters more than the exact angle. Change one thing at a time, or you won't know what helped.

Room layouts

Single room

  • Keep the router and the sensor apart.
  • Let common walking routes cross between them.
  • Keep the sensor out of cabinets and from behind furniture.

Doorway or corridor

  • Place the router and sensor so the route crosses between them, straight or diagonally.
  • Test the whole approach, not only the doorway.
  • Watch for motion leaking in from adjacent rooms.

One per floor or apartment

  • Start with one sensor per floor or apartment.
  • Add another if tests show blind spots or you need motion per room.
  • Name and tune each sensor separately.

Placements to avoid

  • Inside a metal cabinet, behind a television, or pressed against a large metal surface.
  • Right next to the access point, where the signal is strong but covers little of the room.
  • At the edge of coverage, with patchy traffic or RSSI below about -80 dBm.
  • Next to fans, curtains that move in the air, vibrating appliances, or anything else that moves.
  • Where people can easily bump the board, antenna, power cable, or access point.

A repeatable ten-minute test

  1. Check the link

    Check that traffic is steady and, if you can see it, that RSSI is in the good range.

  2. Establish quiet

    With Lightweight, keep the room still while the sensor starts or recalibrates. Then watch the empty room for one or two minutes; the threshold may still drop a little if the first seconds were noisier.

  3. Exercise the target area

    Walk the routes that should trigger motion, including the farthest part of the room.

  4. Check unwanted areas

    Move in nearby spaces that should be ignored, and run the fans or appliances that are normally on.

  5. Change one thing

    Move or rotate the sensor, recalibrate Lightweight, and repeat the same steps. High Accuracy needs no calibration, but still repeat the empty-and-motion check after each move.

  6. Tune only after placement

    Once you have the best spot, make small threshold or filter changes if still needed.

ESPectre detects changes in the radio environment. It does not identify people, cannot guarantee an exact detection area, and does not replace a safety-certified presence sensor.
