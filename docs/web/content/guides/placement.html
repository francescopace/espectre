Place your ESPectre sensor

Choose the sensing link

ESPectre measures changes in a radio link. Signals travel between its two ends directly and through reflections off walls, furniture, and people. Both endpoints and the route people take affect detection.

Access point ↔ ESPectre

  • The usual setup uses the link between the AP and one sensor.
  • Place the sensor so people's routes cross that link.
  • The AP's position determines one end of the sensing geometry.

ESPectre A ↔ ESPectre B

  • One device sends directly to the other.
  • You choose both endpoints, even when the AP is elsewhere.
  • Both devices still need a Wi-Fi connection on the same channel.

A pair lets you try a link across a doorway, along a corridor, or across one part of a room without moving the router. An oval drawn around the two endpoints can help picture the geometry, but it is not a detection boundary. Reflections can carry motion effects beyond it, including from adjacent rooms. Check the actual coverage with the room test.

Placement matters more than small threshold changes. Find a good spot first, then calibrate and tune.

Placement with two ESPectre devices

Use this setup when the AP–sensor path misses the area you care about. For example, put one board on each side of a passage, or place them diagonally across a seating area. Keep both antennas clear and both boards fixed.

  1. Keep both devices on the same channel

    Start on 2.4 GHz. Connecting both to the same AP is the simplest arrangement. On a mesh, choose the same Preferred access point for both in Device settings; sharing an SSID alone does not ensure the same channel.

  2. Set device B to receive

    Connect to B in Live motion. Under Advanced sensing settings, set Wi-Fi traffic source to External device. Find B's Wi-Fi station MAC in your router's connected-client list, matching its IP address. Use that MAC, not the AP's BSSID or ESPectre's device ID.

  3. Point device A at B

    Connect to A, select Wi-Fi raw, and set Destination MAC to Custom MAC…. Enter B's station MAC. Changes save automatically. This requires firmware with destination-MAC support; ESP32-C6 cannot be device A because it does not support Wi-Fi raw.

  4. Check input on both devices

    Keep the default capture profile and packet rate for a first test. With custom builds, use auto capture on both and matching csi_target_pps. Check CSI accepted, CSI occupancy, and readiness on each device. Stop any host traffic generator or Home Assistant traffic-generator add-on feeding B during the test.

  5. Test each detector

    A measures the acknowledgments from B; B measures A's incoming frames. They keep separate motion states. Follow the ten-minute test on each, and repeat it after changing either endpoint.

For capture settings and configuration details, see two-device sensing.

To return to AP-based sensing, set A's Destination MAC to Automatic (AP), and return B to Ping (ICMP) or its previous internal traffic source. Recheck detection after the change.

A good starting placement

  • With an AP–sensor link, put the ESP32 in the area you want to monitor, about 3–8 m from the AP if the layout allows it.
  • With a pair, place the two boards around the area you want to test. Choose spots where people normally walk between the endpoints, and test the approaches and sides too.
  • Keep the antenna end of the board open to the room, away from metal, electronics, and large obstacles.
  • Mount it firmly. A board or cable that swings, vibrates, or gets moved during cleaning causes false motion on its own.
  • Make sure the sensor gets steady traffic, and settle the placement before changing detector settings.

Distance and RSSI

Distances are only a starting point: walls, transmit power, antennas, and furniture all change the link. ESPHome has a WiFi RSSI diagnostic sensor, and Live motion shows RSSI under Diagnostics. The ranges below are useful checks for an AP–sensor installation. For a pair, check accepted CSI and occupancy on both devices, then test movement; RSSI alone cannot establish coverage.

How to interpret the Wi-Fi RSSI observed at the sensor
Observed RSSIWhat it suggestsWhat to do
Above -40 dBmThe receiver may be too close to the access point.Move the sensor farther away before raising thresholds.
-40 to -70 dBmA good range for a first installation.Run the quiet-and-motion test below.
-70 to -80 dBmThe link is weaker, and placement matters more.Test carefully, and prefer a clearer path if results vary.
Below -80 dBmThe signal is weak and more noise is likely.Move the sensor or access point, or remove a heavy obstacle.

Stronger is not always better. A very short link can work worse than a moderate, stable one that crosses the part of the room people use.

Angle and antenna orientation

There is no single best angle. The antenna type, the case, and nearby materials all change the result. Start with this test:

  1. Start clear and facing the link

    Keep the antenna end away from walls and the case, and point the device toward the other end of the link: the AP or the second ESPectre. Keep an external antenna vertical.

  2. Check a quiet room

    Leave the room empty and still long enough to see whether the score stays stable.

  3. Walk the real route

    Walk through the doorway, corridor, or path you want to detect. Waving a hand next to the board is not enough.

  4. Rotate by 90 degrees

    Change only the orientation. With Lightweight, restart the device and let it calibrate with the room still, then repeat the same walk. Keep the orientation where quiet and motion differ most.

The comparison matters more than the exact angle. Change one thing at a time, or you won't know what helped.

Room layouts

Single room

  • Let common walking routes cross the AP–sensor link.
  • If the AP is poorly placed for that route, try two boards across the target area.
  • Keep antennas out of cabinets and from behind furniture.

Doorway or corridor

  • Place the endpoints so the route crosses between them, straight or diagonally.
  • A pair lets you choose that crossing without relocating the AP.
  • Test the whole approach and motion in adjacent rooms.

Several rooms

  • Start with one link and map where it detects motion.
  • Add another only after testing blind spots and unwanted detections.
  • Name and tune each sensor separately; a pair does not create an isolated room or a combined motion state.

Placements to avoid

  • Inside a metal cabinet, behind a television, or pressed against a large metal surface.
  • Both endpoints clustered in a corner, with the usual walking routes outside the link.
  • At the edge of coverage, with patchy traffic or RSSI below about -80 dBm.
  • Next to fans, curtains that move in the air, vibrating appliances, or anything else that moves.
  • Where people can easily bump either board, antenna, power cable, or access point.

A repeatable ten-minute test

  1. Check the link

    Check accepted CSI, occupancy, and readiness. For a pair, check both devices and confirm that they are on the same channel.

  2. Check the quiet room

    With Lightweight, keep the room still while the sensor starts or recalibrates. Then watch the empty room for one or two minutes; the threshold may still drop a little if the first seconds were noisier.

  3. Walk the target area

    Walk the routes that should trigger motion, including the farthest part of the room. With a pair, cross between the boards and approach from both ends and sides. Check each detector's score and motion state separately.

  4. Check unwanted areas

    Walk outside the intended area and in adjacent rooms, even when those routes fall outside an oval around the endpoints. Run the fans or appliances that are normally on. Record unwanted detections as well as missed movement.

  5. Change one thing

    Move or rotate one endpoint at a time, then restart any device using Lightweight: the old threshold may reject the new quiet baseline during manual recalibration. For a pair, moving either board changes the link for both detectors. Let startup calibration finish with the room still, and repeat the same steps. High Accuracy needs no calibration, but still repeat the empty-and-motion check after each move.

  6. Tune only after placement

    Once you have the best spot, make small threshold or filter changes if still needed.

ESPectre detects changes in the radio environment. It does not identify people, cannot guarantee an exact detection area, and does not replace a safety-certified presence sensor.
