Choose your hardware

ESPectre supports six ESP32 chip families. A compact board is usually enough for a dedicated sensor.

Espressif publishes the CSI performance order C5 > C6 > C3 ≈ S3 > ESP32, but does not define the score or test method behind it. S2 is shown sixth to complete this table, although Espressif does not include it in the published ranking. Treat the order as a vendor ranking, not a measured precision value. RX sensitivity is a separate receiver specification: values shown are typical at HT20 MCS0, except for the C5 5 GHz value at VHT20 MCS0. The module, antenna, RF layout, band, and installation still affect measured sensing performance.

What matters

  • The antenna: an external antenna connector (U.FL/IPEX) lets you choose and position the antenna independently of the board. This can help when the enclosure, mounting position, or link distance makes the built-in PCB antenna difficult to place.
  • The power supply: use a stable supply rated for the board; USB-powered boards normally expect 5 V. If readings remain erratic in an otherwise good position, compare them with another power adapter.
  • The placement: keep the sensor out of metal cabinets and, when the room allows it, start 3–8 m from the access point. The placement guide covers distance, orientation, obstacles, and room testing in detail.
  • The programming connection: if the board exposes USB, use a data cable for flashing. Smaller boards may instead require a compatible serial or JTAG adapter.
