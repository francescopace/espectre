PRODUCT ROADMAP · AUGUST 2026

Local sensing, from one device to a system.

ESPectre is moving from a released sensing platform toward easier product integration, optional multi-device orchestration, and future standards-backed Wi-Fi sensing.

Released v3.0 Platform baseline
The foundation is here

One sensing core, multiple ways to ship.

Version 3.0 established ESPectre as a reusable platform rather than a single firmware image.

  • Shared C++ core and runtime
  • ESPHome, Native, Matter, and Streamer frontends
  • Embeddable SDK and stable protocol services
  • Validated Classic and ML detector paths
PRODUCT DIRECTION

Three horizons, one product strategy.

Each horizon builds on the same local-first sensing foundation. Versions indicate sequence, not fixed delivery dates.

01 Now · v3.x

Make the platform easier to adopt.

Outcome: a dependable sensing component for developers, integrators, and device makers.

  • Simpler SDK distribution and developer workflows
  • Broader Matter, hardware, and controller validation
  • Better sensing quality across rates and environments
  • Measured evaluation of displays, Zigbee, TuyaOpen, and peer coordination
02 Next · v4.x

Turn multiple nodes into one sensing system.

Outcome: optional orchestration without making raw CSI a cloud dependency.

  • Secure onboarding, ownership, and fleet visibility
  • Room and device map with near-realtime state
  • Remote settings, signed updates, history, and alerts
  • Local, self-hosted, and managed deployment profiles
03 Later · v5.x

Adopt standards-backed sensing hardware.

Outcome: new radio backends without changing how products consume ESPectre.

  • Track practical IEEE 802.11bf and vendor sensing APIs
  • Map new measurements into the existing runtime contract
  • Preserve protocol, frontend, and tooling compatibility
  • Move only when suitable embedded hardware is available
RESEARCH TRACKS

Measured before promoted.

These tracks may become products, remain research tools, or be rejected. They are not release commitments.

Research

Stationary presence

Separate an occupied but quiet room from an empty one using scale-invariant micro-motion and quiet-floor evidence.

Research

Brief gestures

Preserve short-timescale information at higher CSI rates before evaluating gestures on their own corpus.

Research

Breathing-related motion

Explore micro-motion only after presence is measurable. This remains experimental and is not a medical measurement.
